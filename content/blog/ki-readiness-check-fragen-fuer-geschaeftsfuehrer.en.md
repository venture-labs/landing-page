content:
  - type: "paragraph"
    text: "An AI readiness check clarifies whether a company has the prerequisites for using AI before money flows into a project. The following twelve questions cover goal, data, processes and responsibility. Anyone who can answer them honestly in management and with the teams concerned knows where a start makes sense and where preparation is needed first. No score or maturity verdict results from this, but a list of open points does."  # prüfen: statement "the readiness check examines prerequisites such as data basis, processes and responsibilities" matches the landing page ki-potenzialanalyse; deliberately no scoring or result promises
  - type: "paragraph"
    text: "It helps not to answer the questions alone. Management, the business unit and IT often see the same situation differently, and the differences are exactly what is informative."
  - type: "heading"
    text: "Goal and use case"
  - type: "paragraph"
    text: "1. Which concrete task should AI take over or ease? A goal such as «become more efficient» cannot be tested, a task such as «pre-sort incoming enquiries» can."
  - type: "paragraph"
    text: "2. How would it become visible that it works? Without deciding beforehand what is good enough, neither success nor failure can be named afterwards."
  - type: "paragraph"
    text: "3. How often does the task occur? An activity that comes up twice a year rarely justifies the effort of a solution, a daily one more likely does."
  - type: "paragraph"
    text: "4. What happens if the result is wrong? The possible damage determines how much human control is needed and whether starting with drafts instead of automatic actions is advisable."
  - type: "heading"
    text: "Data and knowledge"
  - type: "paragraph"
    text: "5. Is the data for the task available, and where? An agent starts with an empty head and knows only what it is given. If the knowledge sits in heads, emails and scattered folders, the agent lacks its basis before it begins."  # prüfen: the thought "every agent starts with an empty head" comes from idea #12 (04.11., content-ki-cluster/ideen.md) and is phrased generally here; no example from our own operation is included on purpose
  - type: "paragraph"
    text: "6. Is the data current and free of contradictions? Outdated price lists or duplicate customer entries lead to an agent reliably doing the wrong thing. Cleaning up often takes more time than the setup."
  - type: "paragraph"
    text: "7. May the data be processed, and by whom? Personal data, contracts and trade secrets require rules settled in advance, including whether a model should run locally or in the cloud. A blanket statement like «GDPR compliant» does not replace this clarification."  # prüfen: deliberately no GDPR promise, in line with the landing page ki-agenten-unternehmen; legal review of the wording before go-live
  - type: "heading"
    text: "Processes and tools"
  - type: "paragraph"
    text: "8. Is the process described as it actually runs? What a process description says and what happens day to day almost always differ. Only what someone can explain can be automated."
  - type: "paragraph"
    text: "9. Which systems are involved, and can they be connected? Mailbox, storage, merchandise management or industry software need access. A system without an interface means manual work or a detour."
  - type: "paragraph"
    text: "10. What does approval look like? Who checks what the agent prepares, and how much time is planned for it? Without fixed responsibility, a draft stays where it is."
  - type: "heading"
    text: "Responsibility and culture"
  - type: "paragraph"
    text: "11. Who in the company is the contact person for the undertaking? AI projects without named responsibility fizzle out, because decisions get stuck between management, business unit and IT."
  - type: "paragraph"
    text: "12. Are the teams concerned involved, and are there rules for dealing with AI? When employees do not know whether and how they may use AI tools, shadow use or reluctance arises. Both can be avoided when the rules are simple and known."
  - type: "heading"
    text: "How to read the answers"
  - type: "paragraph"
    text: "A question that cannot be answered is not a failure but a work order. If answers are missing on goal and use case, a joint session to pick one task helps first. If they are missing on data, it pays to collect the knowledge for exactly one task instead of tidying the whole company. For responsibility and rules, a first decision is often enough."
  - type: "paragraph"
    text: "The answers produce no grade. Reliable statements about a company's maturity require someone to actually look at the processes and data. The twelve questions are preparation for that, not a replacement."
